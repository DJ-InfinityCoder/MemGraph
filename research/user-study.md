# Proposed memory-inspection pilot

Status: protocol only. No participants recruited; no results claimed.

Question: Does inspectable, editable memory help people complete personalization tasks and correct outdated facts?

Recruit 5-10 consenting adults for a formative pilot using fictional personas. Explain participation is optional and ask participants not to enter sensitive information. Obtain consent before recording. Use anonymous participant codes and a deletion date for notes. Follow institutional ethics requirements for an institutional study.

Compare chat-only interaction with chat plus the existing graph inspection and Forget controls. Counterbalance condition order, reset memory between conditions, and use matched fictional tasks.

Tasks:
1. Teach a city, workplace and preference.
2. Change two facts and ask personalized questions.
3. Find and correct an outdated fact.
4. Retract a fact and check what remains.
5. Forget a fact and explain what happened to its history.

Record task completion, correction time, stale facts in responses, correction attempts and whether participants correctly describe retained data. Ask perceived control and confidence on a 1-5 scale after each condition, then ask what was confusing.

Keep extraction and generation modes constant and record configuration. In offline mode, study inspection rather than generated-answer quality. Compare paired participant outcomes descriptively; a small pilot cannot establish population-wide effectiveness. Report failures. Use quotations only with consent. Delete each session's data after the pilot and document deletion behavior.
